logging will tell you what people did, but it doesn't say why they did it, that's why we follow up with an **interview** or a **questionniare**

poor question: What is your age?
why?
-  doesn't specify a format - user doesn't know what to say exactly
-  personal question - you shouldn't ask them - have a range instead (mind overlapping values)
-  ticking a box instead of inputting text is better

-  make questions clear and specific
-  ask closed questions and offer a range of answers
-  general questions first
-  avoid complex questions
-  when scale is used, make sure they don't overlap
-  scale should be consistent
-  trade-off: length of the questionnaire

lab testing = "usability laboratory"
-  two way mirror like an interrogation room
-  used in usability evaluation
-  user is watched from the other side and their use and behaviour is captured
-  expensive to build
-  portable usability labs
-  bunnyfoot large observation rooms = they look like living rooms, but have hidden cameras and microphones

## summary of evaluation techniques

* ordered by the level of intrusiveness

asking experts
-  heuristic inspection
observing users
-  indirect observation (interaction logging, diary, etc...)
-  direct observation
asking users
-  think-aloud
-  interview/questionnaire
-  user feedback
asking & observing users
-  lab testing (intrusiveness to users)

lab testing is the most intrusive technique to users (users have to take time to come to the lab and are subjected to heavy observation for multiple hours)
indirect observation is the least intrusive technique to users

measures = what
technique = how
	example:
		**measure:** opinion rating
		**technique**: questionnaire ("how enjoyable was the task on the Likert scale 1-7?")
